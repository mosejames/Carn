Out of the entire job description, one thing has no existing owner: keeping the calendar.

Google Calendar is free. Whatever the answer to scheduling is, it is not $72,799.92 a year.

The question underneath all of this

If the seat needs $72,800 of help, what is the seat for?

Either

This work is already being done by people the city employs.

In which case we are buying it twice, and the council owes the public an explanation of what the second purchase adds.

Or

Whoever holds the seat cannot carry the job the voters elected them to do.

In which case the problem is not a staffing shortage, and hiring someone at three times the salary of the office does not fix it.

Both answers are a problem. Neither one is worth $291,199.68. Voters hired one person to represent Ward 1. Nobody voted for a Chief of Staff, and no one at the city is permitted to supervise the position once it exists.

Three more things worth knowing

It is on the consent agenda.

Section 8 of Monday's agenda is Consent. Section 9 is Regular Business. This is item 8.C, in section 8, which means it passes inside a block vote with no discussion unless a councilmember pulls it out.

One vote authorizes four years and $291,199.68.

The agreement runs to August 31, 2027, then renews itself for up to three more years unless somebody sends a letter stopping it. No stated notice window. No future council vote required. Contract Sec. 2, packet p. 217.

The city pays for it and cannot direct it.

The contract says the contractor shall not be subject to the direction or supervision of any other elected official, City officer, department, employee, board, commission, or authority. The charter gives the city manager administrative control over all regular departments and divisions of the City of College Park. Exhibit A (h), packet p. 226; Charter Sec. 4-7(c).

Monday night

Ask them to pull 8.C from consent.

Every question points at a document, so it is hard to answer with a speech.

  1. If every part of this job already belongs to someone, why does the position exist? And if it does not already belong to someone, say which part, and show it in Exhibit A.
  2. What does this position do that the city is not already paying someone to do? Go down Exhibit A, pages 224 to 226, part by part, and name it.
  3. Which city department was asked whether it could do this work? College Park staffs Procurement, Finance and Accounting, the City Clerk, Customer Service, and Strategic Communications and Public Information right now. Name the ones that were asked, and say what they answered.
  4. Which city employees will report to this contractor? The council memo says the position provides oversight of staff, contractors, and consultants. Charter Sec. 4-7(c) and (d) reserve hiring, firing, and discipline to the city manager and department heads.
  5. Why is a $291,199.68 contract on the consent agenda? Nothing about this is routine. It should be debated in Regular Business and voted on by itself.
  6. Who supervises the position, and under what authority? Exhibit A (h) places it outside every city officer. Has the city attorney put an opinion in writing? The signature line on packet page 223 is blank.
  7. Does a future council have to vote again to keep paying? If the answer is no, then Monday is the only chance the public gets for four years.
