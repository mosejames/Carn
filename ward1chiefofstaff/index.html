Three more things worth knowing

It is on the consent agenda.

Section 8 of Monday's agenda is Consent. Section 9 is Regular Business. This is item 8.C, in section 8, which means it passes inside a block vote with no discussion unless a councilmember pulls it out.

One vote authorizes four years and $291,199.68.

The agreement runs to August 31, 2027, then renews itself for up to three more years unless somebody sends a letter stopping it. No stated notice window. No future council vote required. Contract Sec. 2, packet p. 217.

The city pays for it and cannot direct it.

The contract says the contractor shall not be subject to the direction or supervision of any other elected official, City officer, department, employee, board, commission, or authority. The charter gives the city manager administrative control over all regular departments and divisions of the City of College Park. Exhibit A (h), packet p. 226; Charter Sec. 4-7(c).

The question underneath all of this

If the seat needs $72,800 of help, what is the seat for?

Either

This work is already being done by people the city employs.

In which case we are buying it twice, and the council owes the public an explanation of what the second purchase adds.

Or

Whoever holds the seat cannot carry the job the voters elected them to do.

In which case the problem is not a staffing shortage, and hiring someone at three times the salary of the office does not fix it.

Both answers are a problem. Neither is worth $291,199.68. Voters hired one person to represent Ward 1. Nobody voted for a Chief of Staff, and no one at the city is permitted to supervise the position once it exists.

Before Monday

Questions worth asking.

This item was published Thursday. The meeting is Monday. Consent agendas are not built for debate, and by the time citizen remarks are called the block has usually already been adopted. So this is not a speech to prepare. These are questions worth putting to the council in writing, before the vote or after it.

Open the questions
  1. Does this become the precedent for every other ward? If Ward 1 gets a Chief of Staff, what is the answer when Wards 2, 3 and 4 ask for one? Four of these is $291,199.68 a year, not over four years.
  2. Does the contract outlive the term that created it? Under Charter Secs. 5-8 and 5-9, the current Ward 1 term ends December 31, 2027. This agreement can run to August 31, 2030. That is two years and eight months of a position attached to a seat, past the end of the term of the person holding it.
  3. If every part of this job already belongs to someone, why does the position exist? And if some part does not, say which one, and point to it in Exhibit A.
  4. Which city department was asked whether it could do this work? Procurement, Finance and Accounting, the City Clerk, Customer Service and Strategic Communications are all staffed today. Name the ones that were asked, and say what they answered.
  5. If the work is real, why is it a personal contract instead of a posted city job? A budgeted position gets a public job description, competitive hiring, and civil service. This route skips all of it.
  6. Why was it never competed? The city has a Procurement department and a Purchasing Policies Manual, referenced at Code Sec. 2-23(b). Nothing here tested the price against the market.
  7. Who supervises the position, and under what authority? Exhibit A (h) places it outside every city officer, while Charter Sec. 4-7(c) gives the city manager control over all departments and divisions. The city attorney's approval line on packet page 223 is blank.
  8. Why is a $291,199.68 commitment on the consent agenda at all? Consent is for the routine. A four year obligation that renews itself without another vote is not routine.
