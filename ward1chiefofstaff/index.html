Ten lines. Who already does them?

Exhibit A of the contract spells the job out in nine lettered parts. Each one is below, in bold, exactly as the contract describes it. Slide each bar open to see whose job it already is, then open the drop-down to see how a working city handles that job.

(a)
The contract would pay for this
Runs the council office and advises the officeholder
Whose job it already is
The councilmember
Charter Sec. 1-3. The office has one elected head.
How a working city handles it
There is no separate office to run. The council sets policy, the city manager runs the administration, and the elected official carries their own seat. The advising a councilmember needs comes from the city attorney on legal questions and from department heads on their own subjects, and every councilmember can already ask for it.

Whose job it already is
Finance & AccountingProcurementCity Clerk
Charter Sec. 4-7; Code Secs. 2-3(b), 2-102.

The City Clerk is custodian of official records, and the Code names a Records Management Officer with a retention schedule. Three departments, already funded, already doing this for all four wards.

How a working city handles it
The council elects a city attorney, who puts ordinances and resolutions into legal form so they survive a challenge. Department heads supply the substance in their own subject areas. The councilmember decides what to introduce and casts the vote. If the council genuinely needs more research capacity, the honest fix is a shared analyst serving all four wards, not personal staff for one.

Execution then belongs to the city manager, who is charged with seeing that the business of the city is efficiently organized and handled, and who reports back to council in writing every month.

Procurement solicits and awards. Finance and Accounting reviews invoices against the contract and pays them. The city manager executes contracts and must report every purchase, contract and disbursement to the whole council in writing each month. Keeping invoice review inside the finance department, and away from the politician who wanted the vendor, is the entire point of the arrangement.

Everyone the city pays answers to the city manager, and the city manager answers to the whole council. That chain is the accountability. The question underneath all of this

If the seat needs $72,800 of help, what is the seat for?

Either

This work is already being done by people the city employs.

In which case we are buying it twice, and the council owes the public an explanation of what the second purchase adds.

Or

Whoever holds the seat cannot carry the job the voters elected them to do.

In which case the problem is not a staffing shortage, and hiring someone at three times the salary of the office does not fix it.

Both answers are a problem. Neither is worth $291,199.68. Voters hired one person to represent Ward 1. Nobody voted for a Chief of Staff, and no one at the city is permitted to supervise the position once it exists.

Three more things worth knowing

It is on the consent agenda.

Section 8 of Monday's agenda is Consent. Section 9 is Regular Business. This is item 8.C, in section 8, which means it passes inside a block vote with no discussion unless a councilmember pulls it out.

One vote authorizes four years and $291,199.68.

The agreement runs to August 31, 2027, then renews itself for up to three more years unless somebody sends a letter stopping it. No stated notice window. No future council vote required. Contract Sec. 2, packet p. 217.

The city pays for it and cannot direct it.

The contract says the contractor shall not be subject to the direction or supervision of any other elected official, City officer, department, employee, board, commission, or authority. The charter gives the city manager administrative control over all regular departments and divisions of the City of College Park. Exhibit A (h), packet p. 226; Charter Sec. 4-7(c).

Monday night

Ask them to pull 8.C from consent.

Every question points at a document, so it is hard to answer with a speech.

  1. If every part of this job already belongs to someone, why does the position exist? And if it does not already belong to someone, say which part, and show it in Exhibit A.
  2. Which city department was asked whether it could do this work? College Park staffs Procurement, Finance and Accounting, the City Clerk, Customer Service, and Strategic Communications and Public Information right now. Name the ones that were asked, and say what they answered.
  3. If the work is real, why is it a personal contract instead of a posted city job? A budgeted position gets a public job description, competitive hiring, and civil service protection. This skips all of it.
  4. Why was it never competed? The city has a Procurement department and a Purchasing Policies Manual, referenced in Code Sec. 2-23(b). Nothing tested this price against the market.
  5. Which city employees will report to this contractor? The council memo says the position provides oversight of staff, contractors, and consultants. Charter Sec. 4-7(c) and (d) reserve hiring, firing, and discipline to the city manager and department heads.
  6. Why is a $291,199.68 contract on the consent agenda? Nothing about this is routine. It should be debated in Regular Business and voted on by itself.
  7. Does a future council have to vote again to keep paying? If the answer is no, then Monday is the only chance the public gets for four years. One year with a written performance report would fix that.
