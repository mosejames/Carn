Chapter 1 · The Vendor

Batchelor & Bradley Insights Group.

Batchelor & Bradley Insights Group LLC is a professional-services consultancy led by Amber Batchelor, IOM, its CEO and Managing Partner. The firm describes itself as focused on strategic planning, leadership development, and project management.

We searched every College Park Regular Session packet from January 2, 2024 through June 1, 2026 — 54 meetings across roughly two and a half years. The firm's first appearance in that record is March 2, 2026. Before that date: nothing. After April 6, 2026: nothing so far.

Everything that follows happened inside one five-week window.

Chapter 5 · The Pattern

Six things the record can't explain yet.

01

Same-night approvals, twice

Both contracts were pitched in the Workshop and voted in the Regular Session hours later. No cooling-off period, no follow-up packet, no independent review between pitch and vote — either time.

02

The abandoned RFP

RFP 26-19 was issued, drew responses, and produced no award. The packet contains no written determination explaining the rejection — the only account is the Chief's verbal summary that prices ran high and one respondent was a "one-man shop."

03

Retreat as runway

The firm's fire-study proposal explicitly leverages the retreat: "Because our firm has already worked directly with City leadership…" A $30K relationship contract became the stated qualification for the next $30K contract.

04

No itemized budgets

Neither $30,000 award came with a line-item budget in the public packet. The one line item that was visible — $8,500 for DiSC assessments — drew a pricing challenge from the Mayor.

05

Legal review skipped

The City Attorney confirmed on the record that he had not reviewed the single-source request — then approved of it anyway, seconds later.

06

Scope is already growing

McKenzie, on the record, April 6: "feasibility study for probably all of our departments are going to be coming as well, so, Amber, get ready." A city council member told a vendor to expect future work before the second contract was even voted on.

Chapter 6 · Open Questions

What an Open Records Request would settle.

None of the flags above is an allegation. Every one of them is a documented gap — and every gap has a document that would close it. A Georgia Open Records Act request could produce:

ORR-1RFP 26-19 as issued, all addenda, proof of public posting, and the full list of respondents with their proposals and pricing.
ORR-2Any written determination canceling RFP 26-19 or authorizing the single-source award, and the purchasing policy provisions relied on.
ORR-3The itemized budget Batchelor committed on the record to provide for the $30,000 feasibility study.
ORR-4All communications between any city official and Amber Batchelor or Batchelor & Bradley Insights Group from January 1, 2025 forward.
ORR-5All invoices, payments, and change orders to Batchelor & Bradley to date, including any City Manager add-on spending beneath the $9,999 threshold.
ORR-6The "formal follow-up report" from the leadership retreat that Batchelor said the firm would provide.
